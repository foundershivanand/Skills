Yes. What you’re describing is **not a normal learning platform**. It should behave more like a **personal skill-training operating system**: you choose a measurable outcome, and the system turns it into a progression of drills, tests, levels, and daily missions.

The key design principle should be:

> **Don't teach me about the skill. Train me to perform the skill.**

That distinction is important. Deliberate practice is built around specific performance targets, feedback, repeated attempts, and correcting weaknesses—not simply consuming information. citeturn0search0turn0search7

And your AI-song example fits this philosophy well: instead of teaching "what is AI music?", the platform should give you a mission such as **"Create and publish one complete 2:30 original AI-assisted song today"**, then break that into concrete drills and checkpoints.

# 3. Don't make all skills use the same curriculum

This is critical.

A Rubik's Cube curriculum cannot look like an AI-agent curriculum.

The AI should first classify the skill.

For example:

### Motor skill

`Demonstration → isolated drill → repetition → speed → pressure test → performance test`

### Cognitive skill

`Concept → recall → application → increasing difficulty → timed test`

### Creative skill

`Reference → imitation → constrained creation → original creation → critique → final project`

### Technical skill

`Learn → reproduce → modify → build → debug → independent project`

### Physical endurance

`Baseline → technique → volume → intensity → recovery → benchmark`

### One-hour skill

`10 min learn → 15 min guided → 20 min drills → 10 min challenge → 5 min test`

The AI should automatically choose the appropriate training architecture.

# 4. Every skill needs a measurable definition of mastery

Never allow:

> "Become good at Rubik's Cube."

Instead:

> **Mastery = solve a 3×3 cube in ≤20 seconds for 5 consecutive official-style solves.**

Or:

> **Mastery = memorize a shuffled 52-card deck in ≤5 minutes and recall it with ≥95% accuracy after 30 minutes.**

Or:

> **Mastery = create and export one complete original AI-assisted song containing intro, verse, chorus, bridge, vocals, mixing and final master.**

Or:

> **Mastery = build and deploy an AI agent capable of completing a defined research task without human intervention.**

The platform needs a **Mastery Contract** for every skill.

# 5. The most important object: DRILLS

This is where your platform becomes different.

A lesson might say:

> Learn CFOP.

Your platform should say:

### Drill 01 — Cross Inspection

**Goal:** Reduce inspection time.

**Setup:** Scramble cube.

**Do:**
1. Inspect for 15 seconds.
2. Identify cross pieces.
3. Plan complete cross.
4. Close eyes.
5. Execute.
6. Record time.

**Target:** ≥8/10 correct.

**Failure:** Repeat with 10-second inspection.

**Pass:** 8 successful attempts.

---

That's the type of information the system should generate.

Not paragraphs.

# 7. The brutal AI coach

This should be one of the defining features.

The chatbot shouldn't behave like:

Absolutely not.

It should behave like a demanding coach.

Example:

**You:**

> Target: 30.  
> Result: FAIL.
>
> Your last 5 solves:
> 29.8 / 32.4 / 31.2 / 30.7 / 31.0
>
> Your average is 31.02.
>
> Problem: you're not consistently sub-30.
>
> **Next drill:** 15 solves.  
> Target average: ≤29.5.  
> Maximum allowed: 31.0.
>
> Don't move to the next level.

That's the personality.

# 22. The AI must follow strict rules

Give the coach a hard system prompt with rules such as:

```text
RULE 1
Never overwhelm the learner with information.

RULE 2
Always prioritize action over explanation.

RULE 3
Every session must end with an observable action.

RULE 4
Every drill must have a measurable success criterion.

RULE 5
Never allow progression without passing the required benchmark.

RULE 6
Identify the weakest bottleneck before prescribing more practice.

RULE 7
Prefer drills that isolate the bottleneck.

RULE 8
Do not repeat generic advice.

RULE 9
Do not praise without evidence.

RULE 10
If performance is poor, say so directly.

RULE 11
Never create fake certainty around mastery.

RULE 12
When the learner asks "how do I improve?",

RULE 15
The learner should spend more time performing than reading.
```

That last rule is **extremely important**.
